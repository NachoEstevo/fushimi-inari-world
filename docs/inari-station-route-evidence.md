# JR Inari east exit, Hommachi margin and real shop frontage

## Spatial contract

This packet consumes `inari-station-arrival-spec.mjs`; it does not define a second station coordinate frame. Units are metres, X east, Y up, Z south, with the existing geography origin (34.96714, 135.77267). The source station footprint is `way/952687107`, centre `[-166.7805,37.370]`. Its east entrance is south of the already accepted first-torii crossing: the canonical public exit is approximately `[-163.081224,37.296823]` in XZ, whereas crossing `node/749327076` remains exactly `[-157.407,10.342]`.

The route therefore descends the three photographed front steps, follows the west side of Hommachi north for about 27 m, and turns east through the existing crossing aperture onto existing Omotesando `way/105449722`. It does not draw a diagonal across the opposite parking court or move the inherited first torii. The final new-route waypoint is the existing approach point `[-149.06,10.742]`; the accepted approach beyond that point keeps its own geometry and level ownership.

The inferred public street margin is 1.4 m wide, clear of the mapped building interiors. It joins the interrupted R94 curb at its existing crossing gap. The source-visible south exterior ramp runs parallel to the station frontage. It is 1.2 m wide and 9.3 m long in this interpretation, with a roughly 4.88% slope. Three front steps have approximately 0.149986 m rise and 0.30 m run on the repository's geographic baseline. These are documented appearance estimates, not measurements from a dimensional survey.

## Geometry and floor ownership

`inari-station-route.mjs` builds only missing pavement: the entrance landing, three treads, ramp, disjoint street-margin pieces and the narrow crossing-mouth infill. It clips the margin against the access polygons so it cannot occupy the ramp/steps simultaneously. The retained road/approach tops and crossing paint are not rebuilt. Stair/ramp handrails add actual cylindrical colliders; there are no invisible collision walls across public walking space.

Every pavement level derives from the canonical public floor or the existing Hommachi `surfaces.segments` (`way/79699850` and its southern continuation `way/944841272`). Off-road DEM samples are never used to choose the new pavement top; underlying height is consulted only to bury the visible edge supports. The returned `surfaceHeight(x,z)` samples actual Float32 top triangles through a 2 m spatial index. This matters at inherited road-segment changes, where an analytical function differed from the subdivided rendered pavement by around a millimetre. Walking uses the emitted geometry after that correction. Decorative tactile detail is less than 1 cm above its supporting ramp.

Independent actual-scene review identified a second precision problem: independently subdivided world grids left a microscopic crack at the landing/riser interface, allowing an exact ray to see the terrain below. Adjacent access rectangles now share the same local U/V edge vertices. The reported exact corners and their ±1 cm neighbours have direct ray support after that correction.

The crossing-mouth seam had a different cause: R94's averaged crossing axis did not exactly match the rendered road's segment miters. The final mouth now subtracts the same `pathOffset`/neighbour/width quads used by `buildPathMeshes`, in Float32 coordinates. Three small disjoint pieces end on that actual boundary, with zero overlap area against the road tops. Fourteen rays at the two reported seam points and offsets of ±50 micrometres, ±0.1 mm and ±1 mm hit the new pavement consistently with its walking sampler. This is an ownership correction at the end of the narrow infill, not an overlapping strip or a wider road slab.

The root must query station and new route floor owners before its legacy walking height and must admit new station/route points through its walkability check. Root owns all final integration, generic-building exclusions, collision initialization and actual-scene review. This owner does not edit viewer, index, integration or the existing pavement modules.
