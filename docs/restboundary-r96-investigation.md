# R96 — Omokaru / rear boundary ownership investigation

Inspected production legacy builder, R88 court replacement owner, the current `outputs/r95-integrated/okusha.json` geometry export, its manifest anchor, actual mapped path/building features, and the official exterior/Omokaru photographs. The initial contradiction below was subsequently resolved by primary OSM records; see the implemented resolution at the end.

## Source-backed appearance

The [official Omokaru photograph](https://inari.jp/en/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/omokaru.jpg), inspected as pixels, shows rounded cobble masonry rising behind the two stone lanterns, topped by vermilion narrow vertical fence boards. A timber canopy occupies the foreground. The [official hall photograph](https://inari.jp/en/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/okusyahouhaisyo-1.jpg) also shows cobble masonry and vermilion fencing behind and right of the hall. These prove material/construction vocabulary and relative adjacency; they do not survey coordinates, wall length, height or clearance offsets.

Those are west of the mapped pavilion's front x=258.283…259.323, rather than behind its east sanctuary ending x=272.952. These legacy fixtures and the long east wall are disconnected placements; adding a wall behind the retained stones would introduce a western arrival-side wall without source authority.

## Route contradiction and decision

Actual mapped footway `way/301527762` runs through points (273.782,61.393) to (275.461,72.458). Its centerline passes through the current east wall footprint. That inferred wall is therefore also an unsuitable spatial authority for a new fence. Decorating it would reinforce an already contradictory obstacle on a mapped route.

No wall/fence geometry or conditional cleanup was implemented in this investigation. A coherent next implementation must first resolve the Omokaru/canopy location and the mapped rear passage as one spatial contract. It must either relocate the legacy fixtures with matching interaction/collision ownership, or establish a source-supported physical boundary outside the route's full width and preserve its access. A source-proven material pattern alone is insufficient to choose a new wall alignment.

Protected R95 paving triangles, UV arrays, grading and joins remain untouched. No renderer/browser/GPU attempt was made. Root owns the layout decision and subsequent visual review.
