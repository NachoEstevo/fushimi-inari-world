## Inspected authority

The three supplied Okusha court video stills were directly inspected as pixels:
clipboard IDs `69a82189-e4d8-4f21-819a-b70a07ddd2dd`,
`3ee9aa06-bb02-4d7f-a72c-25c4ed08842b` and
`849b385e-5704-4d67-8012-a4cf247f9377`. The second clearly shows a mixed frontage:
shuttered wooden bays on the left, dark gridded glazing toward the right, pale
cloth above, dark broad eave fascia and fine horizontal grey roof courses.
The first and third show three long straw pendants below the worship pavilion's
rope. These observations, not a generic Japanese-house vocabulary, drive R95.

The existing R88 service building had four identical shutters and individual
blank upper rectangular signs. Its roof was one unarticulated triangle per
slope. The pavilion had a rope and folded paper but lacked the conspicuous long
straw pendants. R94 envelope and bank fixes remain active.

   bays and two recessed dark gridded pane bays. The existing footprint, counters,

   an undocumented interior. Distribution and section dimensions are estimated.
2. Replace the individual blank upper sign blocks with a continuous light cloth
   band and narrow fold relief. No lettering, emblems, paid signage or historical
   inscription is invented.
3. Add a broad dark eave fascia and horizontal roof courses following the exact
   existing eave/ridge slope. The roof outline is preserved.
4. Add the three long straw pendants at the existing mapped pavilion rope. Their
   lowest tips remain above 2.07 m relative to the court datum, clear of normal
   public headroom. No collider or interaction replacement is involved.

The unrelated mapped toilet facade is deliberately untouched because these
photos do not provide authority to give it the reception's glazing or cloth.
The photographs show stone slabs but this packet does not add ornamental seams
on top of the established surface/grade owner; root keeps the route interfaces.
